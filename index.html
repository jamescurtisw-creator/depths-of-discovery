How to Play

Welcome aboard, Captain Cousteau! Explore the ocean depths and become the greatest marine explorer.


Controls

Click water → Move
Click creature → Capture
Click chest → Loot
WASD or arrows → Move

Combat

Engage sea creatures in turn-based capture encounters. Use Stun to weaken the creature, Net Strike for a powerful throw (ready every 3 turns), Defend to reduce incoming damage, or Retreat to escape.


Progression

Capture creatures to earn XP and gold. Level up to increase your stats. Collect gems from rare chests and use gold/gems to Upgrade your boat and submarine.


🏠 Proteus Station

Your underwater base of operations sits on the ocean floor. Swim into it to automatically restore your HP to full. The station has a 5-minute recharge cooldown between heals. Look for the blue marker on the minimap to find it.

About Proteus
🏗️ REAL-WORLD PROTEUS

The Proteus is being built in real life by Jacques Cousteau's grandson, Fabien Cousteau.

The project will cost around $135 Million and will be located in the Caribbean Sea. It is designed to be the world's most advanced underwater habitat — a fully functional ocean-floor research station available to scientists from around the globe.

Inspired by the original Conshelf stations pioneered by Jacques-Yves Cousteau himself in the 1960s, the new Proteus aims to take undersea living into the 21st century.

🤿 THE AQUALUNG

The Aqualung was co-invented by Jacques Cousteau and engineer Émile Gagnan in 1943.

It was the world's first open-circuit self-contained underwater breathing apparatus (SCUBA), allowing divers to breathe freely underwater without being tethered to the surface. This single invention transformed ocean exploration, scientific diving, and Jacques Cousteau's own legendary career.

⚓ COUSTEAU'S LEGACY

Jacques-Yves Cousteau (1910–1997) was a French naval officer, explorer, filmmaker, and conservationist who spent decades exploring and documenting the world's oceans. He co-developed the Aqualung, invented underwater photography techniques, and produced dozens of films bringing the ocean to life for millions worldwide.
